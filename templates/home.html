What does the research involve?

    This study aims to improve and explore the understanding of tax compliance behaviours in the crypto-asset environment. This study is being conducted for academic purposes and it will target crypto-asset users in Indonesia and Australia. It will include an online experiment where you will participate in an asset trading and tax reporting simulation that will last for about thirty minutes, supplemented by pre- and post-activity questionnaires addressing demographic details and tax compliance behaviour. As a token of appreciation, you will receive an e-gift voucher at the end of the online experiment. With your consent, interactions in the online experiments will be recorded and later processed for analysis.

Consenting to participate in the project and withdrawing from the research

    To participate in the study, we will ask you to indicate your consent through a form at the start of the online experiment session. You can withdraw from further participation in the study at any stage.

Possible benefits and risks to participants

    As crypto assets are a new phenomenon in the financial and fiscal landscape, the results of this research will provide benefits to the industry and the authorities in Indonesia, Australia, and internationally by providing an initial exploration of the taxation, governance, and compliance issues. There are no identified risks associated with participating in this project.

Confidentiality

    All information obtained in the online experiment will be strictly used for the purpose of this research only. Any data or references to the online experiment responses reported in publications such as a thesis or journal article will be anonymised and will not be linked in any way to your identity.

Storage of data

    The digital files of the consent form and the online experiment data will be securely stored at Monash University in accordance with Monash University regulations and the Indonesian Law. All digital files will be stored in a password-protected folder. All hard copy documents will be stored in a locked filing cabinet in the office of one of the researchers. Only the researchers will have access to the data, with all data being destroyed 5 years after the final publication. The primary data from Indonesia will be stored for at least 20 years.
